mkTV

Privacy policy

mkTV for Android TV and Google TV, by MKTVHub · Effective 15 November 2026 (mkTV 1.3.5)

In short: mkTV is an ad and tracker blocking web browser. It has no accounts, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no ads or ad SDKs of its own. MKTVHub runs no servers that the app talks to and receives no data about you. What you browse stays on your TV, apart from what the websites you open see, as with any browser.

Data we collect

None. mkTV does not send your browsing, searches, favourites, history or any identifier to MKTVHub or to anyone else on our behalf. We do not sell or share data, because we have none.

What mkTV stores on your TV

In the app's private storage, and only on your TV:

  • Settings (home page, search engine, navigation mode, blocklists you turned on, and similar choices).
  • Favourites, Recently visited (up to 12 sites), History (up to 500 entries) and Continue watching (where you stopped in long videos).
  • Your downloads list (file name, type, size, the site's name, time) and the files you download.
  • Site icons for your Home tiles, and the downloaded blocklists.
  • Parental controls: the PIN as a salted hash (never the PIN itself) and its settings.
  • The phone remote's pairing code.
  • Cookies, cache and site storage, kept by Android's system WebView as in any browser.

You can remove this data with Clear browsing data, the Remove and Clear buttons in mkTV, Android's "Clear storage", or by uninstalling mkTV. Android's device backup may include mkTV's settings, favourites and history, because backup is allowed.

TV home screen. On Android TV, while "Remember where I stopped watching" is on, mkTV gives the title, address and position of Continue watching videos (and your Favourites' titles and addresses for the mkTV channel) to the TV's own launcher, which shows them in Play Next and the mkTV channel. This stays on the TV.

Requests mkTV makes by itself

Everything else is the websites you choose to open. mkTV itself only makes these requests, and none of them carries your browsing history or an identifier:

Blocklist downloadsFor each list that is on, at most about once a day, or when you press UpdateThe list's server (GitHub by default; OISD, HaGeZi or Peter Lowe's list if you turn them on) sees your IP address and Android's standard user agent
Site iconsFor Home tiles without an icon, once per site per session; never in private browsingThat site sees a request for its icon from your IP address
Update checkOnce a day, only in copies installed from GitHub (copies from Google Play are updated by Play and never make it)GitHub sees your IP address and the mkTV version
Search suggestionsOff by default. When you turn them on, as you type in the address bar; never in private browsing, never for addressesThe search engine you chose sees what you type
Video player and downloadsOnly when you start themThe site serving the video or file, with the same cookies and headers the page would send

Searches go to the search engine you choose in Settings (Google by default). Voice search uses your TV's own speech recogniser (on Google TV, Google's speech service); mkTV does not record audio and has no microphone permission.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to load web pages and run the phone remote on your home network.
  • Location (asked only when you press "Fill in automatically" in Share Wi-Fi): Android reveals the connected Wi-Fi network's name only to apps with location permission. mkTV reads only the network name, on the TV, and never reads or sends your location.
  • Install apps: to open an app package (.apk) you downloaded in the browser, and for the update installer in copies from GitHub. Android always asks you to confirm.
  • TV home screen (Play Next and channels): to show Continue watching and Favourites on the Android TV home screen.

Phone remote

The phone remote is off until you first open it. It is a small web page served by mkTV on your local network only while mkTV is on screen, and every request must carry a random pairing code from the QR code. It sends nothing to the internet. It uses plain HTTP on your network, so use it on a network you trust. Settings → Phone remote turns it off, and New code unpairs every phone.

Private browsing

Private tabs keep their cookies and site data separate (on TVs whose WebView supports it) and delete them when you close private browsing. Nothing from a private session is saved to History, Recently visited, Continue watching or site icons. Files you download in private browsing stay on the TV. Private browsing does not hide your traffic from websites, your internet provider or your network.

Children

mkTV is a general-purpose web browser and is not directed at children. It offers parental controls (a PIN, Safe search and blocked sites) for families who want them.

Changes and contact

mkTV is open source, so everything above can be checked in the code. The full technical details are in docs/PRIVACY.md. If this policy changes, the new version and its date are published on this page. Questions: open an issue at github.com/MKTVHub/mkTV/issues.
